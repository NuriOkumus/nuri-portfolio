Building a department its own deployment platform

Five nodes, a full GitOps pipeline, and a permission negotiation at every step.

The problem

Students had nowhere to deploy their coursework. Everyone solved it alone, reaching for whatever external service they could find, and some were paying out of pocket for it. The department owned no infrastructure of its own.

What I did

Nobody asked me to do this. A new DevOps lab was being set up, so I went to my professors and proposed that this be its backbone. They agreed on the spot. I built a five-node K3s cluster. A student pushes code to Gitea and writes their manifests; ArgoCD watches the repo and applies them to the cluster. If an image needs building, Gitea Actions builds it and pushes it to Zot.

Cluster operations

Five nodes, 56 days of uptime, 15 services. I am the only person who keeps them up. The failure I spent the longest on: Overleaf sat in CrashLoopBackOff for two days while MongoDB reported Running and Ready the whole time. The MongoDB pod had restarted with a new IP, and the replica set config on disk still listed the old one, so mongod could not find itself in its own replica set and no primary was ever elected. The readiness probe only pinged the port, so it never noticed.

GitOps delivery

Every service on the cluster is defined in Git. A student writes manifests, ArgoCD reconciles them, and nothing reaches the cluster by hand. Gitea Actions builds images and pushes them to the Zot registry running on the same cluster.

Networking and mail

The hardest part was not technical. Working inside the university network meant getting node IPs pinned, arranging external access, and talking to campus IT every time something was blocked at the network layer. On my own server this would have taken a fraction of the time. Here every step was a permission and a conversation — which is also the job, if the infrastructure already belongs to someone else.

What came of it

The system is up and the full flow has been tested end to end. Students have not used it yet, because the term has not started. Its first real workload is the department’s own website, live on the cluster right now.

What I’d do differently

Settle the network and access permissions first. Leaving them until later meant every technical step ended in a wait.

If something in your infrastructure is stuck, tell me what it is.
